The AFL junior rules are a national handbook that changes how footy is played at each age: smaller grounds and teams for the youngest kids, tackling introduced in stages, and a minimum slice of the game for every player. But the handbook is a baseline. Your league or controlling body sets the exact rules your child plays under.

So treat everything below as the national picture, then check your own league's rulebook for the detail. Ground sizes and team numbers can vary more than you'd expect, and some competitions add their own touches on top.

## What are the AFL junior rules, and who sets them?

The AFL's [Junior Rules Program Handbook](https://play.afl/sites/default/files/2024-06/JuniorRules_May24_Final.pdf) (May 2024) describes the rules as age-appropriate, built on research and best practice, with enjoyment, fun and satisfaction as the focus.

It also says where possible kids should play in single age groups, with a two-year age span as the second option. Several rules, including the exact game-time percentage and scoring from Under 12, are left to the "controlling body". That's the league, association or state body running your child's competition.

## What do the rules look like at each age?

Here are the headline numbers from the handbook for Under 8 to Under 14. "Max" is the most players a team can register, with only the smaller number on the field.

| Age group | Ground (max) | Players on field | Quarters | Tackling | Bounce |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under 8 | 70m x 50m (80 x 60m) | 6 (max 9) | 4 x 10 min | None | None |
| Under 9 | 85 x 65m (100 x 80m) | 9 (max 12) | 4 x 12 min | Modified | 1 max |
| Under 10 | 85 x 65m (100 x 80m) | 12 (max 15) | 4 x 12 min | Modified | 1 max |
| Under 11 | 115 x 75m (130 x 90m) | 12 (max 15) | 4 x 15 min | Full tackling and bumping | 1 max |
| Under 12 | 115 x 75m (130 x 90m) | 12 (max 15) | 4 x 15 min | Full tackling and bumping | 1 max |
| Under 13/14 | 125 x 95m (140 x 110m) | 15 | 4 x 15 to 20 min | Full tackling and bumping | Unlimited |

Quarter lengths are the handbook's play time per quarter. Under 8s through Under 10s also play in three equal zones, which keeps kids in their positions rather than chasing the ball in a pack.

## When does tackling start?

Gradually. Under 8s have no tackling, no holding of an opponent, no fending off, bumping or barging, and no smothering or stealing the ball. At Under 9 and Under 10 the handbook allows modified tackling: holding an opponent is in, while smothering, stealing the ball and knocking it from an opponent's hands are out. Full tackling and bumping arrives at Under 11, along with fending off, smothering and stealing the ball.

## What changes with kicking and marking?

Marks get stricter as kids grow.

- **Under 8:** a mark is awarded to any player who makes a reasonable attempt to mark the ball, at any distance.
- **Under 9 and Under 10:** a mark goes to any player who shows control of the ball.
- **Under 11 and Under 12:** the kick must travel 10 metres and the player must catch it directly.
- **Under 13/14:** the kick must travel 15 metres, again with a direct catch.

Kicking the ball off the ground is not permitted up to Under 12, and is permitted from Under 13/14. Bouncing the ball is not permitted at Under 8, limited to one bounce from Under 9 to Under 12, and unlimited from Under 13/14.

## How much game time does every kid get?

This is the rule most parents ask about. For every age group in the handbook, the wording is: every player must play a minimum of 50 to 75 per cent of game time, with the controlling body determining the exact percentage of game time per player, excluding injured players.

Players should also rotate at least every quarter, so they get a go in several positions, including the interchange.

So there isn't one national percentage. If your child sits more than the league's number, that's a fair conversation with the coach. ## Why no scores for the little ones?

From Under 8 to Under 11 the handbook says no scores, ladders or finals, and no recording of best players or goal kickers. From Under 12 up, scoring and results are the controlling body's call.

It also encourages a mercy rule once a lead is unassailable (it gives 60 points as an example), applied in a way that suits the local competition.

## Where does ScorX fit?

The handbook sets the rules, and your league enforces them. ScorX is your own record of how your child's season actually went. On iPad, ScorX records time played per player and supports rotation planning, which helps coaches see who has had a run. It doesn't check anyone against a minimum, so that part stays with the coach and the league.

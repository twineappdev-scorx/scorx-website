A junior AFL academy is any structured program that adds coaching on top of your child's regular club training, and the word covers three quite different things: club academies, paid holiday or term programs, and the AFL talent pathway. They don't cost the same, they don't select the same way, and they don't promise the same outcomes. Knowing which one you're looking at is most of the work.

This is a grassroots explainer for parents. It isn't a guide to getting drafted, and it isn't a sales pitch for any one provider.

## What are the main types of junior footy academy?

Most programs fit one of three buckets.

**1. Club academies.** Run by a football club or league for its own juniors. Often the lowest barrier to entry, and usually tied to the club's own coaching approach.

**2. Paid holiday or term programs.** Run by AFL clubs, councils, state bodies or private providers. You pay, you book, your child attends. Selection, if there is any, tends to be light.

**3. The AFL talent pathway.** Official state talent programs and club Next Generation Academies (NGAs). These work on selection or invitation, and they are the official route for kids who go on to higher levels.

## How does the AFL talent pathway work?

Entry is by selection, not by paying. State bodies run their own talent programs, and the AFL describes Next Generation Academies as programs run with clubs, with entry by invitation. Rather than relying on a summary from a blog (including this one), read the official pages: the [AFL Next Generation Academies page](https://play.afl/play/next-generation-academies) and your state body's talent page on [AFL Play](https://play.afl/).

Two things are worth knowing for the primary school years. First, the pathway is a long way off for most kids under 12, and nobody is "behind" for not being in it. Second, it doesn't depend on a private academy. If a provider implies otherwise, ask them to show you where the official pathway says so.

## What do paid holiday and term programs offer?

Usually a few days of coaching during the school holidays, or a weekly block in term. They're a good fit for a kid who loves footy and wants more of it.

Examples from public pages include the [AFL Play school holiday programs](https://play.afl/play/school-holiday-programs), club-run academies such as [St Kilda's STK Academy holiday camps](https://www.saints.com.au/news/1806894/our-holiday-clinics-are-back-this-july-secure-your-place-now) and the [Hawks Academy](https://www.hawthornfc.com.au/hawks_academy), and private providers such as [Rookie Me Academy](https://rookieme.com/academy), which runs school holiday football programs and, according to its site, includes testing events as part of its pathway. Each is a different product. Some are a fun day of kicking and games, some add testing, some are built around a club's own coaching curriculum.

## How much time and money does an academy take?

The honest answer is that it varies a lot, and I won't quote a figure I can't point to a current page for. Costs are set by each provider and change by year, region and program length. Check the booking page for the price, and then ask what isn't on it.

The time side is easier to work out. Count the sessions, add travel, and add the hidden bits: a second set of gear, an extra weeknight, a weekend camp. A program that's "just once a week" can quietly take a chunk of the family calendar, on top of club training and Saturday games.

## What should you ask before paying?

Keep this list on your phone.

1. **Who runs it and who coaches?** Ask about qualifications and Working With Children checks.
2. **How are places decided?** First come first served, trial, or invitation?
3. **What does a typical session look like?** And how many kids per coach?
4. **What's the total cost?** Including camps, uniforms, testing fees and any add-ons.
5. **What happens if my child doesn't enjoy it?** Refund and cancellation terms matter.
6. **What does "pathway" mean here?** Ask them to be specific about what the program does and doesn't connect to.
7. **What will we get at the end?** A report, a testing result, a plan, or just a good time.

That last question is the one to press. A kid who gets a clear picture of what they're working on gets more out of any program.

## How do you know if it's working?

Compare against your own child, not the other kids. Keep notes from the first session: what they can do, what they find hard. Come back after a few weeks and see.

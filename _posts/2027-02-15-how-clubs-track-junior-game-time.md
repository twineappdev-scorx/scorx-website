Junior footy clubs track game time in one of three ways: a paper team sheet, a stopwatch and an interchange steward, or a game-day app. Parents usually can't see any of them from the boundary, which is why "everyone gets a fair run" can feel like a bit of a mystery.

If you're searching for a junior footy game time app, the first question is whose job you're trying to do. The club's job is making sure the whole team gets a fair go on the day. Yours is knowing how your own child's season is going. Those are related, but they aren't the same tool.

## How do clubs actually track game time?

Here is what happens on a typical Saturday at a junior club, in roughly the order of how common each method is.

**1. The paper team sheet.** A team manager or coach has a printed list of names and four columns for the quarters. They tick who starts and note changes as they happen. It costs nothing and it works in the rain. Where it struggles is the third quarter, when there are two injuries, a lost mouthguard and a kid who needs the toilet, and the ticks stop.

**2. The stopwatch and the interchange steward.** Some clubs hand one parent a stopwatch and a list. Every few minutes they call a swap and the bench rotates in a set order. It's simple and fair when it works. It gets harder with an odd number of players, a kid sitting out with a sore ankle, or a coach who is also the timekeeper.

**3. A dedicated game-day app.** More clubs now use an app on a phone or tablet where a coach or helper records each change and the app adds up time on ground per player. [Rookie Me Play](https://www.rookieme.com/play/solutions/junior-teams/) is one common example of this kind of club game-day tool. These apps take the maths off a volunteer's plate. They still depend on someone entering every rotation as it happens.

Each method has the same weak point: it is only as good as the person running it, and that person is almost always a volunteer doing three other jobs. For the rules clubs are working towards, see [what the AFL Junior Rules mean for parents](https://www.scorx.com.au/blog/afl-junior-rules-explained-parents/).

## Why can't parents just see the club's numbers?

Usually it isn't secrecy. The team sheet lives on a clipboard, or the app login belongs to the coach and team manager. There just isn't a parent-facing view.

That's a reasonable thing to accept, because the volunteer is already stretched. But if your child has been on the bench more than you expected, the first step is a calm conversation, not a spreadsheet. We cover how to have that talk in [the bench time guide](https://www.scorx.com.au/blog/junior-footy-bench-time-what-to-do/).

It also explains why a lot of parents end up keeping their own notes.
